Data Packages — Why roaming charges hurt so much - and the right alternative

Traditional roaming is an agreement between your home cellular provider and local providers abroad, where the rates are determined by both companies together - and usually not in the consumer's favor. Accessing the internet abroad through standard roaming can easily cost exorbitant rates per megabyte in certain countries, quickly resulting in a bill of hundreds of dollars for normal daily use.

The problem isn't just the price - it's also the lack of transparency. Many travelers don't understand exactly how much they are paying in real-time, discovering the damage only when they return home. The practical solution is to purchase a dedicated international data package in advance, whether it's a local SIM card or a digital eSIM - both offer a known price upfront, a defined volume, and peace of mind throughout the trip.

In recent years, eSIM technology has changed the rules of the game. An eSIM is an embedded SIM card in the phone that can be programmed remotely - meaning, you can purchase and activate an international data package even before arriving at the airport, directly from your smartphone. There is no need to look for a SIM kiosk in a foreign airport, no waiting, and no risk of losing your home SIM card. How to choose correctly - Criteria that save money

Choosing an international data package is not complicated, but it requires attention to a few key parameters. The most common mistake is buying an expensive "global" package when you are actually traveling to only one destination - in many cases, a dedicated package for that country will be cheaper and offer better coverage.

Here are the main criteria you should check before purchasing:

  • Network coverage: Does the provider work with high-quality local networks at the destination? Poor coverage makes a cheap package unusable.
  • Data volume: Think about your real needs - navigation, chat, and social networks consume much less data than streaming video.
  • Validity period: A 7-day package for a 10-day trip is a problem. Make sure the validity covers your entire trip duration.
  • Speed after exhausting the volume: Some packages continue to operate at a reduced speed after the high-speed data is used up - this is a significant advantage.

For example, someone traveling to Brazil can use a dedicated eSIM package for Brazil, and someone planning a trip to Norway will be able to find an eSIM package for Norway that perfectly suits their needs. This approach - a package by destination - usually saves a considerable amount of money compared to a global package.

Pro tips for smart data management while traveling

Even with an excellent data package, proper data management will ensure you don't run out of volume in the middle of your trip. Here is the checklist recommended to go over before and upon arriving at your destination:

  • Turn off automatic app updates - these consume data in the background without you noticing.
  • Set WhatsApp to download media on Wi-Fi only - photos and videos are the biggest data consumers.
  • Use Google Maps in Offline mode - download maps in advance and save data on navigation.
  • Check data usage in your phone settings once every 24 hours so you aren't caught by surprise.

Common mistakes when choosing international data packages

Even experienced travelers fall into some classic traps regarding this matter. Knowing the mistakes in advance saves money and inconvenience in the field:

Mistake 1: Buying a data package without checking coverage at the specific destination - Not every global package covers every country with the same quality. A package offering "coverage in 150 countries" might provide weak connectivity precisely at your destination. Always check the list of local networks the package works with in that country.

Mistake 2: Buying a data package with too short a validity - A two-week trip with a package for only seven days guarantees stress in the second week. Many forget to calculate flight days and add overlap days. Buy a package with a margin of a day or two beyond the planned trip duration.

Mistake 3: Relying on a single data package for a multi-country trip - Anyone touring several countries in one trip should check if the purchased package covers all of them. For example, a trip including Portugal and Romania requires verifying that the package is valid in both countries, or purchasing two separate packages.

Mistake 4: Forgetting to turn off roaming on the home SIM - Even after installing an eSIM, your old home SIM is still active in the phone. If you haven't turned off its roaming capability, the phone might connect to the network through it and accumulate charges. Check your settings to ensure the new eSIM is the active SIM for data.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between an eSIM and a regular SIM card for internet abroad?▼

An eSIM is an embedded SIM card in the phone that can be programmed remotely - there is no need for a physical card, and you can purchase and activate it digitally before traveling. A physical SIM card requires manual replacement of the card in the phone. The big advantage of eSIM is convenience and speed - no waiting at sales booths in the airport and no risk of losing your home card.

Can I use the eSIM simultaneously with my home SIM?▼

Yes, in most modern smartphones you can operate both your home SIM and the eSIM simultaneously. This way you will continue to receive calls and SMS to your home number, while browsing will be done through the cheaper eSIM. Just make sure in the settings that the eSIM is set as the active SIM for data.

How much data do I need for a one-week trip?▼

For average use including navigation, WhatsApp, social media browsing, and checking emails - 3-5GB per week is enough for most travelers. If you plan to watch YouTube or Netflix videos, you should double the amount. It is recommended to purchase a package with a slight margin above your expected consumption.

What should I do if the eSIM doesn't connect at the destination?▼

The first step is to check that the eSIM is set as the active SIM for data in the phone settings. After that, trying to turn the phone off and on solves many connection issues. If the problem persists, you can contact the eSIM provider's support. The eSIM troubleshooting guide details solutions for the most common issues.
