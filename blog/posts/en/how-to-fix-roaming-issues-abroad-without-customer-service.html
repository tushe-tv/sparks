Roaming abroad - what actually causes connection issues abroad?

Roaming abroad is a service where your phone connects to a foreign provider's network through a cooperation agreement with your home provider. When the connection fails, the reason is almost always one of four: incorrect device settings, a network identification issue, a restriction imposed by the provider, or a lack of compatibility between the device and the local network.

It is important to understand that the device doesn't automatically "know" which network to use in every country. According to the guidelines of cellular companies for operating a device abroad, after turning on the device, it takes 5-10 minutes until a suitable local network is found. A large part of the "malfunctions" simply stem from a lack of patience in the first stage.

Common problems identified time and again:

  • Roaming disabled in settings - Sometimes disabled by default to prevent unexpected charges.
  • Incorrect network selection - The device gets "stuck" on a weak or incompatible network.
  • Incorrect APN settings - The access point name for browsing is not configured properly.
  • Roaming package not activated - You forgot to purchase a package before traveling.

Troubleshooting process - 7 steps in order

Go through the following steps in order. Most problems are solved in the first few steps, and there is no need to reach the end of the list.

Step 1: Restart and patience
Turn the device off completely, wait 30 seconds, and turn it back on. After turning it on, wait 10 minutes before any further action. The local network needs time to identify the card.

Step 2: Checking roaming settings
On iPhone: Settings - Cellular - Cellular Data Options - Data Roaming. On Android: Settings - Network & Internet - Mobile network - Roaming. Make sure the option is enabled.

Step 3: Manual network selection
Go to network settings and choose "Manual Network Selection". The device will scan and display a list of available networks. Choose the leading network in the country you are in - it usually appears with a clear name.

Step 4: Manual APN configuration
If browsing still doesn't work after connecting to a network, the APN must be configured manually. Search Google for your home provider's name + "APN settings" + the country name. Enter the details in the cellular network settings.

Step 5: Check provider restrictions
Send a short SMS message to your home provider (short numbers like 900 for Cellcom, 054-service number for Partner) and ask for confirmation that the roaming package is active. Sometimes the package is not activated automatically.

Step 6: Network type compatibility check
Some countries operate on frequencies that are not supported by all devices. In the network settings, try switching between 4G, 3G, and 2G. Sometimes dropping to an older network solves the problem.

Step 7: Consider an eSIM alternative
If steps 1-6 did not solve the problem, the fastest solution is purchasing a local eSIM. An eSIM is a digital SIM card installed directly on the device that connects you to a local network - independent of your home provider's roaming agreements.
